Why does screening matter?

Screening translates the tones and colours in your artwork into printable dot patterns. The selected screening can affect detail, gradients and unwanted patterns such as moiré. Use settings suited to the intended printing conditions.

What do the screening settings control?

Tonal value controls the amount of ink in the pattern. Screen ruling, dot shape and screen angle describe other properties of that pattern.

How fine is the dot pattern?

Screen ruling is commonly expressed in lines per inch (LPI). A higher ruling places more, smaller dots in the same physical area at the same tonal value.

The nominal tonal value stays at 40%.

LPI is different from DPI. Output resolution (DPI) describes the imaging grid used to build the halftone dots. Image resolution (PPI) describes the pixels in the source image. A higher LPI is not automatically the right production choice.

How do the dots develop?

A dot shape defines how the pattern develops from highlights through midtones to shadows. Similar-looking highlight dots can develop differently later in the tonal range.

Why rotate the separations?

In conventional AM screening, colour separations commonly use different angles to help control interference between their overlapping patterns. Unwanted interference can appear as moiré.

The combined pattern may form a rosette. Use the angle set approved for your workflow.

One separation is shown to make rotation clear. This is not a recommended CMYK angle set or a moiré simulation.

Why can settings differ between print jobs?

The same artwork can need different screening settings when the printing process, plate or material changes.

Offset

Plate → blanket → material

Ink transfers from the plate to a blanket and then to the material. Screening must suit the intended press, plate and paper conditions.

Flexo

Anilox → raised plate → material

An anilox roll meters ink onto the raised printing areas of the plate. Screening must also work with the anilox pattern and the plate’s ability to reproduce small highlight dots.

In practice: a photograph is used on an offset-printed carton and a flexo-printed pouch. Identical artwork does not mean identical screening settings. Check the approved specification for each job.
